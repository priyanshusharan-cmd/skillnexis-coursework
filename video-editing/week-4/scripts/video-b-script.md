# Script B: "I designed an expense app in 7 days (and what I got wrong)"

* **Format:** long-form, about 7 minutes, scripted but not yet produced. It is the "next video" promised in video A's
  end screen.
* **Audience:** students curious about UI/UX, and anyone who liked video A.
* **Goal:** the second step in the channel's funnel. Video A brings in viewers through a money problem; video B shows
  the process, for viewers who want depth.

| Part | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| HOOK | 0:00–0:20 | Open on the finished prototype, then rewind: "Seven days ago this was a notes-app ledger." |
| BODY | 0:20–6:15 | Five days, five chapters. Each chapter states a decision, shows the evidence, and shows the result on screen. |
| CTA | 6:15–7:00 | "Try the prototype (link)". "Tell me what you'd change". Subscribe; end screen points to video A. |

---

## HOOK (0:00–0:20)
**VISUAL:** a fast montage of the 18 final screens (2 beats each), then a hard cut to a messy notes-app screenshot.

> This app took me seven days to design. Research, wireframes, eighteen screens, and a prototype you can click.
> And on day five, I found out my favourite feature was a terrible idea.
> Let's rewind.

**ON SCREEN:** "7 days · 18 screens · 1 bad idea".

## BODY

### Day 1: The problem isn't maths (0:20–1:20)
**VISUAL:** presenter, then the UPI statistic as a kinetic stat card, then the journey map with its lowest point
highlighted.

> Everyone told me the hard part of splitting money is the maths. It isn't. Paying is instant now, with UPI.
> The hard part is remembering, and then asking. Look at this journey map. The lowest point isn't paying.
> It's the moment you have to message a friend about a hundred and twenty rupees.

**B-ROLL:** competitor screenshots (blurred brand names), and the comparison table from the case study.

### Day 2: One number per friendship (1:20–2:30)
**VISUAL:** greyscale wireframes being laid out, then a side-by-side of the home screen before and after.

> My first home screen showed every expense. Nobody cares about every expense.
> People care about one number: what do I owe, or what am I owed? So that's the hero now. Plus eight fifty four.

**ON SCREEN:** the principle "One number per relationship".

### Day 3: Ten seconds or it won't happen (2:30–3:40)
**VISUAL:** a screen recording of the add flow with a stopwatch overlay, sped up with a ramp.

> If adding an expense takes longer than paying for it, people stop adding them.
> So the amount pad opens first, and everything else has a smart default. Four taps. About ten seconds.

### Day 4: Colours that don't lie (3:40–4:40)
**VISUAL:** a contrast checker with pass and fail chips, then a simulation of red-green colour blindness on the
balances.

> Red for "you owe" and green for "owed to you" is a classic. It's also invisible to about one in twelve men.
> So every amount also says it in words. And every colour pair passes WCAG AA. I checked all fifteen.

### Day 5: The feature I killed (4:40–5:40)
**VISUAL:** a mock-up of "auto reminders", a big red X, then the real reminder screen with the three tone options.

> Here's the bad idea: automatic reminders. Hisaab would nudge your friends for you, every three days.
> Then I imagined being on the receiving end. That's not a reminder. That's a debt collector.
> So reminders are user-sent, from your own WhatsApp, in a tone you choose. The app never nags behind your back.

**ON SCREEN:** "Design for the person receiving it, too".

### Days 6–7: Prototype and testing plan (5:40–6:15)
**VISUAL:** the prototype walkthrough GIF, the axe-core report showing 0 violations, and the test plan as a checklist.

> Days six and seven were the prototype, an accessibility audit, and a plan for five usability tests with hostel students.
> I haven't run those yet, so treat everything here as a hypothesis.

## CTA (6:15–7:00)

> The prototype link is in the description. Tap through it, and tell me in the comments what you'd change.
> If you missed it, the money habits video is on screen now. And subscribe, because next I'm testing this with real students.
