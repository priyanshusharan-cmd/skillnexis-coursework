## [0:47] Habit 1: Log it when you pay — BODY

**PRESENTER (green screen, café background)**

> Habit one. Log it the moment you pay.
> Not tonight, and not at the end of the month.
> Memory is the most expensive part of splitting money. Every expense you add later is one you might get wrong.
> We tested this in our room. Expenses added on the spot were always right. Expenses added at night were wrong about one time in five.

**SCREEN: prototype, add flow**

> In Hisaab, adding takes about ten seconds.
> Open your room's group, and tap the plus button.
> The amount pad opens first, because the amount is the one thing you always know.
> Paid by defaults to you, and the split defaults to everyone in the group.
> So most of the time, you type a number and hit save.

## [1:29] Habit 2: Split fairly, not equally — BODY

**PRESENTER (green screen, café background)**

> Habit two. Split fairly, not equally.
> Equal splits feel simple, but they quietly annoy people.
> If Aarav was at the library during the Maggi run, he shouldn't pay for it. And he shouldn't have to argue about it either.
> Fair means everyone pays for what they were part of. That's it.

**SCREEN: prototype, add flow**

> So the split screen starts with everyone ticked, and you untick whoever wasn't there.
> Hisaab recalculates instantly. A hundred and twenty each, three people.
> And it shows that the total still adds up to three sixty, so nobody has to double check the maths.
> For rent or a trip, you can switch to exact amounts, shares, or percentages.

## [2:10] Habit 3: Pick one settle day — BODY

**PRESENTER (green screen, café background)**

> Habit three. Pick one settle day.
> Ours is Sunday evening.
> When settling is a routine, asking for money stops being personal. It's just Sunday.
> It also keeps the amounts small. Settling ninety rupees every week is easier than five hundred at month end.

**SCREEN: prototype, settle flow**

> On settle day, open home and tap settle up.
> You see exactly who you're paying, their UPI ID, and what it's for.
> Pick any UPI app and confirm. The money goes bank to bank. Hisaab never holds it.
> Your balance updates straight away, and you can clear the next person in the same sitting.

## [2:48] Habit 4: Let the app ask — BODY

**PRESENTER (green screen, café background)**

> Habit four, the friendship saver. Let the app do the asking.
> Nobody wants to be the person sending, hey, about that hundred and twenty.
> But a neutral reminder, with the exact amount and a pay link, doesn't feel like nagging.
> The app becomes the bad guy, and you get to stay the friend.

**SCREEN: prototype, extras flow**

> Tap remind, and pick a tone. Friendly, direct, or funny.
> The message goes from your own WhatsApp, so you decide when it's sent.
> Hisaab never sends automatic reminders behind your back. That was a deliberate design choice.

## [3:50] 3 mistakes to avoid — BODY

**TEXT CARD: 3 mistakes to avoid**

> Before we wrap up, three mistakes we made, so you don't have to.
> One. Rounding everything up. Ten rupees here and there adds up, and someone always notices.
> Two. Letting one person become the room's bank. They end up owed thousands, and too shy to ask.
> Three. Settling only when someone is broke. By then the amounts are big, and the conversation is awkward.

**PRESENTER (green screen, café background)**

> All three have the same fix. Small amounts, logged right away, settled on a fixed day.

## [4:22] Recap — BODY · recap

**TEXT CARD: Recap**

> Quick recap.
> Log it when you pay. Split fairly. Settle on one day. Let the app ask. And know where it goes.
> None of these are about maths. They're about making money boring, so friendships don't have to absorb it.
