How to split hostel expenses with roommates (without fights)

Shared rooms run on small, frequent payments: a late-night food order, the Wi-Fi recharge, a cab to the station. Paying is easy with UPI; keeping track is what causes arguments. Here is a simple system that works for any hostel or PG room.

1. Agree on what is shared

Write down, together, which costs are split: Wi-Fi, room supplies, shared food orders, cleaning. Personal things (your own snacks, your phone recharge) stay personal. Ten minutes on day one saves a month of "but I never ate that".

2. Pick the fairest split for each kind of cost

CostFair method
Wi-Fi, room suppliesEqual split
Group food ordersOnly people who ate; equal among them
Cab ridesEqual among riders, or by distance if drop-offs differ
TripsBy shares (someone skipping a day pays fewer shares)
Electricity with an AC userBy percentage agreed upfront

3. Record it the moment you pay

Memory fails by the weekend. Add the expense when you pay, with a short name ("Maggi run, 11 pm") so everyone recognises it later.

4. Keep one running balance, not a list of debts

If Diya owes you ₹120 and you owe her ₹80, the only number that matters is ₹40. A running balance per person, simplified across the group, turns ten small payments into two or three.

5. Settle monthly, on a fixed day

Pick a day, say the 1st, when everyone clears their balance by UPI. A fixed day makes asking feel like routine, not nagging.

Doing it with an app

You can run this system on a shared spreadsheet. Or use an app that keeps the balances for you: Hisaab lets you add an expense in seconds, split it any of the ways above, and settle with any UPI app.

Quick checklist

  • Agree what's shared on day one
  • Choose a split method per cost type
  • Record expenses when you pay
  • Track one balance per person
  • Settle on a fixed day each month
