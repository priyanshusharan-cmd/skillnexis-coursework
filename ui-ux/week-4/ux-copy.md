# UX copy: Hisaab onboarding (3 screens)

**Voice:** a sorted friend from the hostel. Plain, warm, a little playful, never preachy about money.
**Rules:**
* Say the benefit first, the feature second.
* One idea per screen.
* Titles ≤ 6 words.
* Body ≤ 25 words.
* Name the worry before the user has to ask about it (money safety, awkwardness).
* "You" and "your friends", never "users".

| Screen | Title | Body | Button |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Value | No more “who paid for what?” | Add a shared expense in seconds. Hisaab keeps a running balance with every friend, so nobody has to remember. | Next |
| 2 · How it works | Split it any way that's fair | Equally, by exact amounts, or by shares when someone skipped dinner. Everyone sees the same numbers. | Next |
| 3 · Settle | Settle up in one tap | Pay what you owe with any UPI app. Hisaab never holds your money and never sees your UPI PIN. | Get started |

## Why these words
* **Screen 1** opens with the exact question every hostel group chat asks. Recognition beats explanation. "Nobody
  has to remember" names the real job: removing the mental load and the awkwardness of keeping score.
* **Screen 2** pre-empts the main objection to splitting apps: "equal isn't fair when I didn't eat". The concrete
  case ("someone skipped dinner") does more than listing four split modes. "Everyone sees the same numbers" answers
  the trust question.
* **Screen 3** addresses the scariest part, money, in the body text and not in fine print. UPI is named because
  it's what every student already trusts; "never sees your UPI PIN" is a promise the product design actually keeps.
* **Buttons** are verbs with no "Continue" ambiguity. The last one says "Get started" because the next screen asks
  for something (a phone number). "Skip" sits top-right on screens 1–2 but not on 3, so nobody skips the money promise.

## Alternatives considered
| Screen | Rejected | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | “Track group expenses easily” | Feature-speak; "easily" is a claim every app makes |
| 1 | “Friendship-saving expense tracker” | Cute, but overpromises and sounds like an ad |
| 2 | “Equal, exact, shares or percentages” | A list, not a benefit; percentages rarely matter to students |
| 3 | “Secure UPI payments” | Vague; "secure" doesn't tell people what we don't do with their money |

## Microcopy elsewhere (same voice)
* **Sign in:** “We'll text a 6-digit code. Friends find you by this number, so splits reach the right person.”
  This explains *why* we need the number.
* **Saved:** “Saved. Diya, Kabir and you were notified.” Confirms who now knows.
* **Empty group:** “Nothing here yet. Add the first expense, and the maths is on us.”
* **Reminder:** “We never send reminders automatically. You decide when.” Avoids the awkwardness students fear most.
* **Payment:** “Paid directly bank to bank. Hisaab never holds money.”
